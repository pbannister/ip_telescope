<h1>Project status</h1>

<p>
<b>IP_telescope</b> collects information about IPv4 addresses that are
curious: addresses whose answers, or whose silence, do not match what the
registry says should be there.
</p>
<p>
The first curiosity is a number trick. An <i>ip_probe</i> is an IPv4 address
whose four octets are each prime and which is valid for routing across the
Internet, such as <code>2.2.2.2</code>. There are exactly 7,400,808 of them.
The second curiosity is the quiet space: 49,572 of those addresses sit in
blocks that no Regional Internet Registry operator holds, where nothing is
supposed to answer at all.
</p>

<h2>Status</h2>

<p>
Phases 1, 2, and 3 are complete. Phase 3 ran on 2026-09-10: 49,572 probes in
space no operator holds, each asked one HTTP question. Counts below verified
2026-09-10.
</p>

<ul>
<li>Phase 1 — <code>dataflow.out/01_ip_probe.json</code> — 7,400,808 probes.</li>
<li>Phase 2 — <code>dataflow.out/02_ip_block.json</code> — 9,200 blocks holding probes (8,616 operator-held, 584 not), each with its RIR RDAP record.</li>
<li>Phase 2 — <code>dataflow.out/03_ip_probe.json</code> — 7,351,236 probe-to-block pairs.</li>
<li>Phase 2 — <code>dataflow.out/04_ip_probe.json</code> — 49,572 probes no operator holds.</li>
<li>Phase 3 — <code>dataflow.out/05_ip_probe_http.json</code> — 49,572 observations: 99 answers, 6 refused, 1 reset, 49,466 timeouts.</li>
<li>Phase 4 — <code>dataflow.out/06_ip_probe_characterize.json</code> — 342 targets: 106 anomalies and 236 non-prime control addresses.</li>
</ul>

<p>
Every answer came from a block the registry calls <i>reserved</i>. The
loudest is <code>23.191.149.0/24</code>, inside ARIN block
<code>23.191.137.0-23.191.151.255</code>: 98 probes answered with an nginx
<code>301</code> to HTTPS. RDAP records no operator for that block either —
only its parent <code>23.0.0.0/8</code>, which ARIN holds itself. Six
addresses in reserved APNIC and ARIN blocks refused the connection, and one
of those blocks (<code>199.47.160.0/21</code>) has no RDAP object at all.
</p>

<h2>What phase 4 found</h2>

<p>
The four-prime pattern did not survive contact. Every service answered its
non-prime control neighbours too, so the pattern was an artifact of where
phase 3 looked, not a property of the services. The control sample turned 99
answering addresses into 237 in one block.
</p>

<ul>
<li><b>23.191.137.0-23.191.151.255</b> (ARIN, <i>reserved</i>) —
<b>incidental</b>. The answering range is exactly the eight <code>/24</code>s
announced by AS400050, and <code>ology.com</code> resolves into it: an
ordinary web host with a stale registry record. Its certificate expired in
May 2025.</li>
<li><b>103.241.72.0/22</b> (APNIC, <i>reserved</i>) — two claims.
<b>Incidental</b> for the anomaly (the <code>/24</code> is announced by a
hosting provider while the record says reserved) and <b>nefarious</b> for the
activity: an automatic redirect page, a throwaway domain farm in the
certificate, and no reverse DNS.</li>
<li><b>103.149.23.0/24</b> (APNIC, <i>reserved</i>) — <b>incidental</b>. The
<code>/24</code> is announced by StarHub; the refusals are ordinary filtering
on an announced range with no service.</li>
<li><b>199.47.160.0/21</b> (ARIN, <i>reserved</i>, no RDAP object) —
<b>unexplained</b>. Resets come from an announced <code>/24</code> inside a
block with no registry object, and the device is not identified.</li>
</ul>

<p>
The evidence, the verdicts, and the measurement that would overturn each one
are in <code>records/01-probe-characterization.md</code>. The expected
characterization was written first, in
<code>documents/07-characterization-theory.md</code>.
</p>

<p>
The light-speed test found nothing either. A round trip to geosynchronous
orbit takes 239 ms at best and to the Moon 2,564 ms; no address measured
anywhere near either. The smallest connect to the ARIN site was 8 ms, which
rules out both distances outright, and the refusals at 170–181 ms agree with
an operator on the ground. The five-second budget put the lunar floor inside
the window, so the 89 targets that stayed silent are evidence against the
Moon too. What remains unseen is everything slower than five seconds: Sun–Earth
L2 at ten seconds, and Mars at minutes.
</p>

<p>
The RDAP enrichment of the 9,200 blocks answered for 8,556 and returned
<code>404</code> for 27; 617 were left unanswered because
<code>rdap.afrinic.net</code> was unreachable and the other registries rate
limited the run. Those addresses are not treated as answered, so
<code>make enrich</code> with <code>--retry-failed</code> asks again.
</p>

<p>
Run <code>make probes</code>, <code>make blocks</code>,
<code>make enrich</code>, and <code>make observe</code> to reproduce each
phase; see <code>README.md</code> and <code>PHASES.md</code>. Every work
product that already exists is kept rather than rebuilt: the phase 3 pass
costs nineteen minutes of live probing, so <code>make all</code> with
everything present finishes in seconds and writes nothing.
</p>
