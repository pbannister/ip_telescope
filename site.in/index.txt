<h1>Project status</h1>

<p>
<b>ip_telescope</b> collects information about IPv4 addresses that are
curious: addresses whose answers, or whose silence, do not match what the
registry says should be there.
</p>
<p>
The first curiosity is a number trick. An <i>ip_probe</i> is an IPv4 address
whose four octets are each prime and which is valid for routing across the
Internet, such as <code>2.2.2.2</code>. There are exactly 7,400,808 of them.
The second curiosity is the quiet space: 49,572 of those addresses sit in
blocks that no Regional Internet Registry operator holds, where nothing is
supposed to answer at all.
</p>

<h2>Status</h2>

<p>
Phase 1 (probe generation) and phase 2 (block collection) are complete;
phase 3 (probe observation) has its tooling built and waits on the full run.
Counts below verified 2026-09-10.
</p>

<ul>
<li>Phase 1 — <code>data/01_ip_probe.json</code> — 7,400,808 probes.</li>
<li>Phase 2 — <code>data/02_ip_block.json</code> — 9,200 blocks holding probes (8,616 operator-held, 584 not).</li>
<li>Phase 2 — <code>data/03_ip_probe.json</code> — 7,351,236 probe-to-block pairs.</li>
<li>Phase 2 — <code>data/04_ip_probe.json</code> — 49,572 probes no operator holds.</li>
<li>Phase 3 — <code>data/05_ip_probe_http.json</code> — not yet written.</li>
</ul>

<p>
Run <code>make probes</code>, <code>make blocks</code>, and
<code>make observe</code> to reproduce each phase; see <code>README.md</code>
and <code>PHASES.md</code>.
</p>
