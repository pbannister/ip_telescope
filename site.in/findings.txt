<h1>Observed ip_probe results</h1>

<p>
This page records what the project has actually observed: which addresses
answered, what answered there, and what the evidence supports. The
<em>expected</em> characterization — the four explanations and the tests that
separate them — is written down separately, in
<a href="documents-07-characterization-theory.html">the characterization
theory</a>. The full evidence and reasoning are in
<a href="records-01-probe-characterization.html">the phase 4 record</a>.
</p>

<p>
Everything below is dated. Nothing here is a conclusion about intent: it is
what the measurements show, and what would overturn each reading.
</p>

<h2>The control sample, and a correction to our own theory</h2>

<p>
Phase 4 probed non-prime control addresses beside every anomaly, because the
four-prime pattern is our pattern, not necessarily anyone else's. The
controls answered as readily as the probes did: 99 answering addresses became
237 in one block.
</p>

<p>
The first reading of that result was wrong, and the project owner corrected
it on 2026-09-11. The draft argued that a party running the inverse exercise
would answer only on the four-prime addresses, so answering the neighbours
argued against that explanation. The owner's objection: a party watching a
range would answer its whole range, and our probes would simply have fallen
inside a block they monitor. That is the more sensible expectation, and it
means the control sample separates nothing between a curious party and an
ordinary one.
</p>

<p>
What the control sample does settle is the extent of each live range, and
that no service exists <em>because</em> of the prime pattern. Separating a
watcher from an ordinary host needs the awareness test: a response that
changes because of our probing, a baseline from a second vantage point, or
content that engages the pattern. None has been observed yet.
</p>

<h2>The light-speed test</h2>

<p>
A round trip cannot be faster than light, so distance is the one measurement
an Earth-bound explanation cannot fake. One round trip takes 239 ms to
geosynchronous orbit and 2,564 ms to the Moon. The project measures single
round trips rather than whole requests, because a request costs two round
trips and a responder at lunar distance would need 5.1 seconds to answer a
<code>GET</code> while completing a connect in 2.6 seconds.
</p>

<p>
No address measured near either quantum. The smallest connect to the ARIN
site was 8 ms, which rules out geosynchronous orbit by a factor of thirty and
the Moon by a factor of three hundred; the refusals at 170–181 ms agree with
an operator on the ground in Singapore. Jitter ran from 1 ms to 1004 ms with
no steady address, which is what a long terrestrial path looks like.
</p>

<p>
The five-second budget put the lunar floor inside the window, so the 89
targets that never answered are evidence against the Moon as well as the ones
that did. What remains out of sight is Sun–Earth L2 at ten seconds and
beyond, which is the outer edge of the range the project means to look at.
</p>

<p>
A caution the project now records: a latency band is not a signature. Thirty-one
of the 99 phase 3 answers fell within a quarter of the geosynchronous quantum
and every one was ordinary long-haul routing. Satellite broadband subscribers
sit in that band legitimately.
</p>

<h2>Stability</h2>

<p>
Two characterization passes ran 21 minutes apart on 2026-09-11. All 241
answers, all 241 page digests, and every control outcome were identical: not
one address changed state. Stable behaviour is what ordinary services look
like, and it is mild evidence against a moving operation. A 21-minute window
is a first data point, not a proof.
</p>

<h2>What is still open</h2>

<ul>
<li>The awareness test needs a second vantage point on another continent: a
terrestrial server answers a nearby observer faster, a distant one does not
care, and the project has one host.</li>
<li>Site <code>199.47.167.0/24</code> stays unexplained until the resets are
attributed: a traceroute or an outside measurement would place the device.</li>
<li>Live ranges are sampled, not mapped to the address.</li>
<li>Phase 5, deferred: rogue <code>ip_probe</code> inside operator-held
blocks, where an address answers although the registry's own object does not
cover it. Large blocks are the likely hiding places.</li>
<li>The criteria for "curious" remain provisional. They changed once already,
on the owner's correction.</li>
</ul>
